### L-004 (EXAMPLE) Price tests on landing pages overstate willingness to pay
- Area: Business › pricing · Type: Avoid · Class: public
- When: before setting a launch price from a landing-page or survey test
- Observation: a "pre-order at $X" button got a 6% click rate; at launch, 0.8% paid. A second product that asked for a refundable deposit saw 2% deposit and 1.7% paid.
- Cause: a click costs nothing; paying does. Stated intent drops sharply once money moves.
- Action: test price with a step that costs something (a deposit, a paid pilot); treat click-only rates as an upper bound.
- Limits: deposit tests need a payment setup and can scare off early users. Two products is a small denominator; rival explanations: different audiences, different launch timing.

### L-005 (EXAMPLE) Annual plans offered at checkout lower early churn
- Area: Business › customers and churn · Type: Do · Class: public
- When: designing the first paid plan or the checkout page
- Observation: one vendor case study reports lower 90-day churn after adding an annual option at checkout. No counter-case found yet.
- Cause: a prepaid year removes the monthly cancel moment; the case study does not separate this from a discount effect.
- Action: offer an annual option next to monthly at checkout; compare 90-day churn of both cohorts before keeping it.
- Limits: one vendor source about its own product (thin). A discount may be the real cause. Mirror test: if churn had risen, the same story could blame the commitment.
